# Lifeboat: voice-over script

Record yourself reading these lines, on camera, in **one continuous recording**. You don't need a
perfect take: if you stumble or don't like a line, pause and say the whole line again. The edit
keeps the best take of each line, removes the pauses and mistakes, and fits the screen recording
to your timing. About 410 words: roughly 3 minutes once cut.

## How to record
- **Camera:** landscape (16:9), at eye level, your face in the middle with some space above your
  head (the edit crops a circle from the centre). Face a window or a lamp; avoid a bright window behind you.
- **Sound:** a quiet room. A phone or earbuds mic held 20–30 cm from your mouth beats a laptop mic
  across the room. Fans and AC off.
- **Pace:** calm and conversational, like explaining to a friend. Don't rush; pauses get cut anyway.
- **Between lines:** stop for about **2 seconds**.
- **Retakes:** if a line goes wrong, stop, pause 2 seconds, and **start that line again from the
  beginning**. Say a line as many times as you like: by default the last clean take wins.
- **Don't read the line numbers aloud.** They're only there to keep your place.
- Look at the camera when you can; glancing down at the script is fine.
- **Send the original file** (MP4 or MOV), not a copy compressed by WhatsApp or Telegram.

## Scene 1 · The problem
1. Every Ethereum wallet is protected by a key.
2. The first time you send a transaction, part of that key, the public key, becomes visible to everyone.
3. Today, that's fine. But a large enough quantum computer could turn that public key back into your private key, and empty your wallet.
4. Lifeboat gets you out before that day comes.

## Scene 2 · Check an address
5. First, check any address. This one is exposed: it has sent thousands of transactions, so its public key is out there.
6. And the check itself is private. It goes through a mixnet, so nobody learns which address you looked up.

## Scene 3 · Move
7. Here's a practice account in the same situation.
8. Lifeboat makes it a new set of quantum-safe keys, a thousand and twenty-four of them, right here in the browser. They never leave this device.
9. Then one click creates the new account and moves everything into it, the ETH and the tokens, in a single transaction. All of it moves, or none of it does.
10. And it's free: a sponsor pays the fee.
11. Ethereum's normal mempool refuses this transaction. The new alternative mempool lets it in.
12. Done. The old account is empty, and here it is on the network's explorer.

## Scene 4 · Use it
13. This is the new account. Every payment is signed twice: with the old key, and with one of the new quantum-safe keys.
14. Each orange square is a key that's been used once, and is now retired.

## Scene 5 · Try to break in
15. Now let's pretend that day has arrived. We hand an attacker the old private key, exactly what a quantum computer would recover.
16. On a normal wallet, that's game over.
17. The attacker signs a transaction to drain the account, and fakes the quantum-safe part. Refused.
18. They try something smarter: copying a real quantum-safe signature from an earlier payment. Also refused.
19. Each signature only works for the one transaction it was made for. The money stays put.

## Scene 6 · Turn off the old key
20. Today the account asks for both keys.
21. When the threat is real, one switch turns the old key off for good. From then on, only the quantum-safe keys count. And payments keep working.

## Scene 7 · Public log, and close
22. If you choose, your move is listed on a public log on Arkiv, so the record outlives this test network.
23. You don't have to trust it: one click checks it against the real transaction.
24. Quantum-safe signatures already exist. What didn't exist was a way to get your account there.
25. Lifeboat is that way. Check, move in one free transaction, and stay safe. Just move before the quantum computers arrive.

## What happens to your recording
1. Your audio is transcribed on this machine with timestamps (nothing is uploaded anywhere).
2. Every take is matched to its line. Per line, the last complete take without a stumble is kept
   (tell me if you want a specific take instead: "line 9, the second one").
3. Silences and false starts are cut, leaving natural short gaps. Your voice gets light noise
   reduction and is evened out to standard online loudness.
4. Each scene of the screen recording is stretched or trimmed to the length of your lines for it,
   so the picture always matches what you're saying.
5. Your face goes in a circle in the bottom-right corner, and the result is exported as one
   1080p MP4, 3:00 or shorter.
