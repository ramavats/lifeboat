# Lifeboat: voice-over script

Read this out loud on your phone camera, in one go. It's written the way you'd say it, so
say it like you're showing a friend, not reading a statement. **You don't need a perfect take**:
if a line goes wrong, pause and say that line again. Send the whole recording, unedited; the
edit keeps the best take of each line and cuts everything else. 430 words, about 2:50.

## Before you hit record
- **Phone sideways (landscape)**, propped up at eye level, about an arm's length away. The back
  camera looks better than the selfie camera if you can frame yourself; either is fine.
- **Your face in the middle** of the frame, with a bit of space above your head (the edit cuts a
  circle from the centre).
- **Light in front of you** (face a window or a lamp), never a bright window behind you.
- **Quiet room**: fans and AC off, phone on Do Not Disturb. Wired earbuds with a mic help a lot.
- **Do a 10-second test** and play it back: can you hear yourself clearly? Then record for real.

## While recording
- Read a line, then **pause for about 2 seconds**.
- **Messed up? Pause, then say the whole line again** from its start. As many times as you like;
  the last clean take of each line wins unless you tell me otherwise.
- **Don't read the numbers or scene titles out loud.** They're just to keep your place.
- Smile when it fits, look at the lens when you can. Glancing at the script is fine.
- When you're done, **send the original file** (from Files or AirDrop, or Google Drive), not a
  copy squashed by WhatsApp or Telegram.

## Scene 1 · The problem
*On screen: the LIFEBOAT wordmark builds itself.*

1. So, every Ethereum wallet is protected by a key.
2. And the first time you send a transaction, part of that key, the public key, becomes visible to everyone.
3. That's fine today. But a big enough quantum computer could turn it back into your private key, and empty your wallet.
4. Lifeboat gets you out before that happens.

## Scene 2 · Check an address
*On screen: checking a famous address.*

5. Let's check an address. This one's exposed. It's sent thousands of transactions, so its public key is already out there.
6. And the check itself is private. It goes through a mixnet, so nobody knows which address I looked up.

## Scene 3 · Move
*On screen: the practice account moves everything.*

7. This new kind of account isn't on mainnet yet, so here's a practice account in the same spot.
8. Lifeboat makes it a thousand and twenty-four quantum-safe keys, right here in my browser. They never leave this device.
9. Then, one click. It creates the new account and moves everything into it, the ETH and the tokens, in one transaction. Either all of it moves, or none of it does.
10. And I don't pay a thing. A sponsor covers the fee.
11. Here's the interesting part. Ethereum's normal mempool actually refuses this transaction. The new alternative mempool lets it through.
12. Done. The old account's empty, and here it is on the explorer.

## Scene 4 · Use it
*On screen: the new account and its key grid.*

13. This is my new account. Every payment is signed twice: with my old key, and with one of the new quantum-safe keys.
14. Each orange square is a key that's been used once. After that, it's retired.

## Scene 5 · Try to break in
*On screen: the attacks get refused.*

15. Now let's pretend that day is here. I'm handing an attacker my old private key, exactly what a quantum computer would get.
16. On a normal wallet? That's game over.
17. So the attacker signs a transaction to drain the account, and fakes the quantum-safe part. Refused.
18. They try something smarter: copying a real quantum-safe signature from one of my earlier payments. Also refused.
19. Each signature only works for the one transaction it was made for. The money stays put.

## Scene 6 · Turn off the old key
*On screen: the switch to quantum-safe only.*

20. Today, the account asks for both keys.
21. When the threat is real, one switch turns the old key off for good. From then on, only the quantum-safe keys count. And payments still work.

## Scene 7 · Public log, and close
*On screen: the public log, then the home page.*

22. If you want, your move can go on a public log on Arkiv, so there's a record even after this test network resets.
23. And you don't have to take my word for it. One click checks it against the real transaction.
24. Look, quantum-safe signatures already exist. What didn't exist was a way to actually get your account there.
25. That's Lifeboat. Check your address, move in one free transaction, and stay safe. Just move before the quantum computers show up.

## What happens to your recording
1. Your audio is transcribed on this computer, with timestamps. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.
2. Every take is matched to its line, and the best one is kept (the last clean take, unless you
   say otherwise: "line 9, the second one").
3. Silences, stumbles and restarts are cut, leaving short natural gaps. Light noise reduction,
   and the volume is evened out to standard online loudness.
4. Each scene of the screen recording is fitted to the length of your lines for it, so the
   picture matches what you're saying.
5. Your face goes in a circle in the bottom-right corner. Out comes one 1080p MP4, 3:00 or shorter.
